Known-but-deferred issues. Each entry states what is wrong, what is currently true in the reader, and why it wasn't fixed at the time it was found. ## Schedule clause eId collisions: see `lex-au/repo/FUTURE.md`, not lost Part/Division grouping

This repo previously carried (in `build/bundle.py`'s `_EID_DISAMBIG` comment) the claim that colliding schedule-unit eIds come from the converter "flattening schedule Part/Division numbering" / dropping "the source AKN's `<part>`/`<division>` grouping". That framing is wrong on two counts: there is no source AKN (the source is DOCX), and that grouping never existed in the converter's output to be dropped. The real mechanism, and the numbers, are in `../lex-au/repo/FUTURE.md`'s corrected entry (P1 investigation, 2026-09-07): ~98.7% of the 2,831 colliding eIds are `_CLAUSE_RE` / SECTION-branch false positives (amendment-instruction items, embedded schedule TOCs, un-wrapped quoted provisions), and genuine Part/Division boundary loss is only ~1.2%.

**Resolved 2026-09-27 (Task 22, v0.4.3).** The converter-side fix (B4) landed in `lex-au` v0.10.0 and this repo's `build/bundle.py` `_schedule_units`/`build_toc`/`build_sections` now recurse through the new `amendedAct`/`item`/`quotedStructure` nesting instead of flattening it. The `~N` schedule-unit eId-disambiguation workaround (`_EID_DISAMBIG`'s use in `_add_entry`) is retired — B4 eliminated that collision at the source, so one reaching `_add_entry` today is a converter regression and raises instead of being papered over. See `.superpowers/sdd/2026-09-08-wfw-v0.3.1-phase2-4/progress.md`'s Task 22 entry for the full rationale, including two collision-axis rulings the removal required (a still-legitimate body-level head-note collision axis, and a `<body>`-scoping fix for the per-section walk).

## DOCX footnotes are not extracted (6 Acts, international-instrument schedules)

lex-au's converter reads `word/document.xml` only (`docx_reader.iter_paragraphs`, `fidelity.docx_paragraphs`); `word/footnotes.xml` is never consumed, so footnote text in the source DOCX is invisible to both the converter and the fidelity audit, and never reaches this reader. Registered as known-limitation (f) in `../lex-au/repo/docs/known-limitations-schedule-and-conversion.txt` (P7 investigation, 2026-09-07) and accepted as a named carve-out rather than fixed for v0.3.1 — see the parity claim in `../lex-au/repo/README.md`.

Scope: substantive footnotes exist in exactly 6 Acts, all inside Schedules that reproduce an international instrument — `crimes-(currency)-act-1981`, `cross-border-insolvency-act-2008`, `international-fund-for-agricultural-development-act-1977`, `marine-insurance-act-1909`, `national-environment-protection-council-act-1994`, `psychotropic-substances-act-1976`. 3 of the 12 footnotes are weakly operative (glosses, reservations, cross-references qualifying a reproduced provision); 3 are explanatory. Zero endnotes and zero operative header/footer content corpus-wide (both were also investigated by P7 and ruled out as gaps).

If a future checkpoint decides to extract these, the minimal shape (from the P7 note) is: on the converter side, `docx_reader.py` opens `word/footnotes.xml` alongside `word/document.xml`, builds a `{w:id: note_text}` map (skipping `separator`/`continuationSeparator`/`continuationNotice` boilerplate types), and emits an AKN `<authorialNote marker="N">` at each `<w:footnoteReference w:id="N">`'s inline position inside the schedule content; `fidelity.py`'s `docx_paragraphs`/`akn_paragraphs` need a mirrored footnote-text branch so the audit doesn't register the newly-converted text as a spurious divergence. On this repo's side: the bundle build and reader would need to render `<authorialNote>` content (inline marker + note body, likely a footnote-style popover or endnote list per section, matching the hover-definition interaction pattern already used for defined terms). Effort estimate: M-L. Not started; no task currently scheduled for it.

## Duplicate body `<section>` eIds silently lose content (79 Acts, ~104 sections)

`build_sections`'s main per-section walk (`build/bundle.py`, `for section in body.iter(f"{AKN}section")`) writes into `sections` via a plain dict comprehension keyed by eId, with no collision handling at all — unlike the schedule-unit walk (`_add_entry`, asserts on B4-era duplicates) and the head-note walk (`_add_headnote_entry`, disambiguates with a `~N` suffix for the real, still-occurring body Part/Chapter numbering-reuse case). A duplicate body `<section>` eId silently last-write-wins: the earlier section's content is permanently dropped from the bundle, with no error and no trace.

Found by final code review of Task 22 (2026-09-27), while checking whether the `_add_entry`/`_add_headnote_entry` collision-axis split was complete — it isn't; this is a third axis with no guard. Pre-existing (not introduced by Task 22 or by B4): confirmed via a corpus census that 79 Acts have duplicate body-level `<section>` eIds, losing 104 sections total (e.g. `australia-(request-and-consent)-act-1985` loses `sec-1`/`sec-2`/`sec-3`; `aboriginal-land-rights-(northern-territory)-amendment-act-1987` has `sec-1` three times).

Not fixed in Task 22's patch pass: it is unrelated to B4/schedule content, predates this plan, and (per the reviewer's own recommendation) deserves its own small task with a proper corpus census in the ledger rather than being folded into an unrelated fix pass. The fix shape is likely straightforward — route the body walk through `_add_headnote_entry`-style disambiguation (or a dedicated variant) instead of the bare dict comprehension — but the choice between "disambiguate both and keep" vs "assert, since two body sections sharing an eId within one Act is arguably always a converter defect" needs its own investigation into whether either of the 79 Acts has a legitimate reason for the repeat (the head-note axis's Corp Act/ITAA-97 precedent suggests it might not be as clear-cut as the schedule-unit axis). Not started; no task currently scheduled for it.
